# R06 ongoing-stream lifetime pilot

Prospective T039 comparison, after the [streaming semantic gate](../results/R06-streaming-entry.md). The decision is whether current conditional and specialized explicit execution have credible ongoing-query lifetime costs, and which retained state would need a different ownership model. [R07 selection](../results/R07-architecture-checkpoint.md) prioritizes this over a narrow finite output rerun and the broader coarse-region parallel alternative.

H1: conditional query-scoped birth/support and occurrence history grows after delivered outputs are released, raising service and disposal cost despite constant-size semantic continuation. H2: explicit execution also retains growing branch lineage and possibly terms/history; it is not a presumed bounded-memory winner. H3: consumer-retained answers explain some growth but cannot explain a drop-policy runtime slope. H4: finite exhaustion may leave query-owned state until disposal; cancellation must release it too. Growth selects ownership diagnosis and, where consequential, reclamation or a representation change. Small stable overhead strengthens bounded applicability. No universal architecture ranking follows from this intentionally low-sharing stream.

Use exactly `tests/stream_support/mod.rs` fixtures. Families `ground` and `aliases`; extents `unbounded` and `finite`; targets16,64,128; consumers `drop` and `retain`; backends `conditional` and inferred `specialized` Global/Indexed. This is48 cells. The unbounded generator emits identical constant-size complete answers and one continuing alternative; finite depth n emits exactly n successful raw answers and a failing zero continuation. Nonground answers preserve joint residual aliases. No deduplication. Ground is the cleanest history witness; finite input syntax and fresh locals in aliases introduce known term-lifetime differences. Do not attribute their growth solely to choice history.

One fresh-process warmup, five primary repetitions, one allocation run and one work run per cell:384 processes. Random(39040) shuffles within each mode. Serial execution on one inherited allowed CPU; no simultaneous comparative jobs. Release primary uses `--no-default-features --features experiment`; allocation uses `--no-default-features --features alloc-meter`; work uses `--features experiment`. Use separate target directories. Capture compiler/host, source and binary hashes, commands, outputs and failures; verify source freeze after all runs. Allocation meter self-check must pass. No native compilation timing claim.

Charge source cloning/lowering in preparation, query cloning/setup, service through complete answer observation, consumer drop or retained-vector insertion, and engine/prepared/output disposal. Original source fixtures and fixed measurement buffers remain alive outside the measured baseline; subtract that baseline for retained requested heap. Preserve per-answer measurements. Validate each full answer with the independent allocation-free checker between service and consumer intervals. Subsequent intervals are validation-warmed. Timing is the sum of measured intervals; it is not a continuously uninstrumented stream. Record first delivery and per-answer service latency; tick counts are service granularity, not equivalent logical work across engines. Instrumented timing is not primary evidence. Requested heap is not RSS. Engine disposal includes any unfinished continuation; finite exhaustion is checked exactly. Final live requested heap must return to the baseline after all measured owners are disposed.

Resource bounds:20million total service ticks/process,30seconds/process,1GiB address-space and30minutes overall after builds. Child failures, service caps, timeouts and outliers remain in raw results and prevent unqualified ranking of that cell. Build failures stop before comparisons; a source change requires renewed freeze. Do not resize or replace cells during this comparison. Serialize after measured intervals. No oracle evaluator runs in measured intervals and no scalar reference dependency is introduced.

Analyze all cells: primary median/range of total charged lifecycle, preparation/setup, first delivery, service latency by prefix, consumer cost and disposal. Compare requested live heap after consumer handling, peak requested heap and allocation traffic, subtracting baseline and separating retained-output controls. Compare finite pre-drop retention with post-drop restoration. Use source-derived ownership bounds plus diagnostic counts to explain growth; at three sizes this is evidence of measured growth, not a fitted asymptotic theorem. Overlapping ranges do not establish timing preference. Large monotone retention or latency growth despite released outputs warrants a causal ownership investigation; mere small timing differences do not justify tuning. Investigate failures before using affected evidence. Mixed-phase composition, compilation amortization, broader sharing, eager-region eligibility and parallel granularity remain distinct open questions.

# Integrated local lifecycle sizing against source-qualified compiled controls

This is exploratory sizing, not confirmation or complete architectural selection. The preceding four-package breadth review selects it because source capability, counter-free execution and ownership are now qualified. It can show whether local repair and integrated bodies change complete costs enough to warrant a broader integrated architecture trial; repeated dynamic reunion and resource-aware lowering remain distinct required alternatives.

## Hypotheses and controls

H1: useful shared activation can repay local incident/dependency maintenance relative to generic and equally specialized compiled execution. H2: indexed equality dependencies can reduce low-yield repair, but their registration/retention costs can dominate at small sizes. H3: recursive body execution exposes representation/observation costs that candidate counts alone miss. H4: preparation reuse changes total costs and must be measured over changed queries.

Seven controls: local Endpoint, Filtered and Indexed; compiled Global generic Scan and Indexed; compiled Global source-specialized Scan and Indexed. The latter uses the qualified leading occurrence plus distinct nullary partner property. No source has OR. Local and compiled paths execute the same complete rule/query, including residuals, fresh aliases and consumption. An independent scalar evaluator supplies complete raw answers outside measurement intervals.

Four source families: recursive constructor bodies with posted consuming calls and residual links; flat independent ground repeated-pattern requests; blocked repeated-pattern requests enabled by a shared constructor binding; low-yield repeated-pattern requests whose shared unknown is aliased to another unknown without resolving their independent counterparts. The final two families use the same rule body to produce the enabling/repair equation, not host-triggered work. Every query has named outputs for its observable variables. Changed queries vary ground labels or variable identities.

## Prospective matrix and bounds

Sizes 4, 32, 128; prepared reuse 1 and 4 changed queries. This gives 24 source/lifetime cells and 168 control cells. Use five isolated ordinary-allocator counter-free timing processes per control cell (840 processes), two separate counter-free requested-allocation processes (336), and two separate diagnostic work processes (336). No in-process warmup: preparation/setup are primary endpoints and the experiment measures fresh process instances; build/loading are outside the measured phases. Randomize each repetition block with fixed seed 7204. Preserve full commands and order.

Add allocation-only cancellation checks at size 32, reuse 1, across four families and seven controls: after setup and after one engine service operation, twice each (112 processes). That operation is not equal work across engines; these checks establish restoration and expose charged cancellation phases, not a cancellation-speed ranking. Total planned processes: 1624, following semantic/measurement smoke checks.

Each process is limited to 60 seconds and 1 GiB address space by the driver. Each execution has a 200000-service-operation bound and complete output is required. A cutoff is unfinished evidence requiring diagnosis, never a loss. Source and binary hashes, toolchain and CPU affinity are recorded before the matrix. Runs are sequential to avoid competing measurements.

## Endpoints and interpretation

Time preparation (including source-plan import/clone as actually required), setup, execution, complete observation, query disposal, answer release and prepared-plan disposal separately. Prepared rules are reused across queries. The deterministic single-answer sources have coincident first/full observation; report their interval once and derive first-answer latency from setup/execution/observation. Source construction, oracle generation/validation, diagnostic serialization and result serialization stay outside timing/allocation phases. Record output retention through query disposal.

Primary total is the sum of those measured phases, not elapsed process time. Allocation traffic similarly sums phase readings. Report peak requested live heap from phase peaks relative to the prepared-session baseline, and separately check final restoration. Requested bytes are not RSS. Outer restoration intervals are not cost totals. Ordinary timing uses no allocation meter and both engines' counters disabled; work and meter elapsed times are excluded from timing interpretation.

Report medians and ranges, phase attribution and exact paired diagnostic/allocation replay. Five repetitions support sizing and candidate follow-up, not a universal winner or a confirmatory significance claim. Investigate consequential opposite regimes, correctable defects or overlap. A component result must survive stronger source elimination, broader heads/history, language restrictions and sustained lifetime before supporting a whole-architecture choice. Compilation is not independently isolated here.

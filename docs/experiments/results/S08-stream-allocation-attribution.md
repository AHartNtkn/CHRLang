# Body and application work dominate conditional stream allocation

Body execution and application checking account for most requested allocation in the conditional stream witness. Observation and discovery are much smaller, even though every answer is exported completely. The next attribution should examine support operations inside the dominant stages before adding reclamation or interpreting this implementation's cost as intrinsic.

## What was measured

The [registered diagnostic](../registrations/S08-stream-allocation-attribution.md) runs ordinary and inferred-unary conditional execution on alias and distinct streams at depths16/64. Each cell completes one query and releases every answer immediately. Two repeats give16 processes and eight exact replays. Every run first validates full answers against the independent scalar evaluator and analytical expectations outside measured ownership.

A read-only, allocation-feature-only method identifies which existing branch the next engine tick will execute. Meter intervals around ticks attribute requested bytes/calls to observation, discovery, notifications, bodies, applications and scheduling. Stage sums exactly equal the whole execution requested bytes and allocation calls. Fixed owner snapshots allocate nothing; they record populations at answers1/4/16/64 and exhaustion. The query/prepared owners restore the root baseline in every cell.

These nested meter intervals reset the peak tracker. Their peak fields are not whole-run peaks and are not interpreted. This package measures allocation traffic and owner populations, not timing or per-owner heap sizes. The preceding [cross-query pilot](S08-cross-query-ownership.md) remains the source for unperturbed peak and lifecycle readings.

Unary dispatch substantially reduces discovery traffic but does not address the dominant body/application work. It moves some posting-related allocation into the body stage, so stages must be interpreted alongside the total rather than independently ranked.

Distinct outputs raise observation traffic to1,909,801 bytes and body traffic to51,718,576 bytes in the inferred mode. Application traffic remains34,890,644 bytes. The larger output still does not dominate allocation on this source. At depth16, inferred alias body/application traffic is1,499,256/1,627,924 bytes. These two depths show sensitivity; they do not establish an asymptotic growth law.

## What remains owned

At depth64 exhaustion, both dispatch modes retain16,986 support nodes,130 bindings and130 change records. There are392 occurrence records, all with false live supports. History and pending-body counts are zero. Inferred dispatch retains only two index entries and one discovery key; ordinary dispatch retains392 entries and391 keys. Neither has queued discovery at exhaustion.

These counts narrow the ownership question but do not prove earlier reclaimability. Occurrence identity, conditional bindings and support handles participate in source effects and observation while the query is live. Zero live occurrences at exhaustion does not authorize clearing them at an earlier answer. The cross-query experiment already proves complete engine disposal releases its state.

The support implementation gives a concrete next hypothesis: each Boolean apply job owns its continuation and memo table, while the arena retains canonical support nodes across jobs. Repeated body and application support operations may reconstruct expensive temporary work. This is code-based attribution direction, not a measured explanation of all stage bytes. Equality matching, binding updates and frame construction also allocate within these stages.

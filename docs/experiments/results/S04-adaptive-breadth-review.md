# Next test sustained execution and consumer pressure

Select T074 to extend lifetime evidence beyond bounded query batches into sustained service with independently varied consumer retention and demand. Current resource-phase results show lower live memory alongside higher allocation and faster execution; adaptive checks show that fewer attempts can change subsequent work. Neither determines which complete path remains economical over a long-lived workload.

The strongest alternative is adaptive separation's ownership/cost comparison. It is now implementable and required. Sustained service receives the next package because it can change conclusions across several viable organizations and exercises a missing obligation, rather than refining one new policy. This is a priority judgment, not evidence against adaptation.

## Concrete lifetime entry

First audit the existing stream and cross-query runners so the experiment extends their evidence rather than constructing another baseline. Reuse competent compiled, graph/contextual and applicable reuse/lowering paths with independent source answers. Establish which can offer the same consumer-visible service before comparing their costs.

Separate stable-size changing queries from growing-source controls. In the stable-size stream, vary identities and relevant values while keeping required work/output size bounded; increasing stream length can then distinguish a retention plateau from accumulated engine history. Growing-source streams must retain their changing required-work explanation. Neither final disposal nor an increasing problem size proves sustainable per-query memory.

Cross immediate release, a bounded answer window and retained-all consumers with consumer demand in bursts. Specify what advances when the consumer pauses, where backpressure acts, and whether work continues beside a pending answer. Include cancellation and reuse, a finite answer beside ongoing work, repeated versus unique work, and independently checked complete finite queries or finite observations of continuing sources. Do not equate a cutoff with exhaustion.

Keep preparation/artifacts, engine history and consumer answers as separate owners. Compare reclamation, bounded retention and regeneration when applicable; expose unsupported publication/continuation contracts instead of silently narrowing the language. A materialized phase result is not automatically an interruptible stream. Qualify those endpoints before selecting lengths, repetition counts and cost thresholds prospectively.

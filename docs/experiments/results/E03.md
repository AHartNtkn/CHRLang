# E03: common expansions survive, but services can dominate

Both v1 and corrected v2 pass all 314 registered case/mode runs, and each complete fresh-process replay reproduces every non-time field. The finite-support engine preserves the registered observations and shares physical expansion after explicit choice. It also demonstrates a substantial adverse case for eager compatible-group search. This result does not select a production representation.

## Reproduction and validation

Registration: [E03](../registrations/E03.md). V1 source/registration freeze: `3f49fa3`; v2 correction and preregistration: `a933df0`. Build with `cargo build --release -p chr-conditional --example conditional_probe`; execute `python3 research/chr-conditional/run_batch.py <output-path>` twice. Each case/configuration runs in a fresh release process, with a 60-second bound. Environment: rustc/cargo 1.97.1, Linux 6.8.0-138-generic x86_64. Elapsed fields are diagnostic.

Evidence: [v1 batch](E03-conditional.tsv), [v1 replay](E03-conditional-replay.tsv), [v2 batch](E03-conditional-v2.tsv), [v2 replay](E03-conditional-v2-replay.tsv). All natural and discriminating grid application/expansion counts satisfy the preregistered formulas. V2 differs from v1 only in term copies, allocation calls, requested bytes and elapsed time; source, scheduling, support and retained-memory counters agree exactly.

The 61 workspace tests pass, as do Clippy with warnings denied and formatting. Conditional tests compare all 64 E00 observations in both modes, check live invariants after every tested quantum, check unchanged projections outside served groups, and exercise later-enabled propagation history. These are independent expected-answer and invariant checks, not a full transition simulation proof or generated-trace investigation.

## Sharing and its costs

For k=8, W=256 and no unrelated constraints, Singleton performs 65,791 physical expansions; Ready performs 264. Both represent 65,791 source applications. Requested allocation falls from 2,346,508,843 to 1,409,962,749 bytes. Peak live payload above baseline falls from 27,921,170 to 3,427,955 bytes. Physical occurrence, event and work-node sharing is real within this engine.

The services still do substantial projected work: both modes preview 132,357 operations, examine 66,047 matching candidates and change 287,694 support memberships. Ready performs 9,174,448 term visits versus Singleton's 329,749, including witness comparisons during grouping. Both retain 139,776 support memberships. A reduction to k+W expansions therefore does not establish k+W total work or compact symbolic support.

With 128 unrelated occurrences, peak increments are 242,032,434 and 217,537,955 bytes. Full materialized answers dominate much of the retained payload. Ready requests 1,629,326,125 allocation bytes, while the E01 persistent control requests 362,703,614 for that workload. This is contrary evidence to treating expansion sharing as sufficient efficiency. The engines differ in owned versus interned terms, indexing, supports and histories; the comparison identifies costs to investigate, not an isolated causal speed ranking.

## Negative case and defect investigation

In the constructor-discriminating k=4, W=64, noise=16 case, Ready saves only common choice preparation: 1,028 versus 1,039 expansions. Its repeated attempt to find compatible ready work increases previews from 2,145 to 32,865 and matching candidates from 8,975 to 140,563. Requested allocation rises from 13,587,425 to 133,225,395 bytes; peak increment is identical at 748,896. The grouping policy needs an eligibility/indexing or scheduling comparison. Simply increasing sharing aggressiveness is not supported.

V1 also exposed quadratic answer-resolution copying: a depth-128 ground term copied 8,385 nodes to produce 129 nodes. A test established that defect before repair. V2 uses borrowed contextual dereferencing during resolution and copies each output node once. At k=8, W=0, noise=128, Ready's requested allocation falls from 4,605,829,813 to 221,553,333 bytes, with every non-allocation/source result unchanged. Peak retention is unchanged because the full returned answers are still retained. This intervention prevents an avoidable adapter cost from being attributed to the representation.

## Disposition and follow-ups

The first finite-support mechanism probe is complete; E03 remains open. BDD/region supports, indexed or incremental matching, lineage/generated-transition checks, resumable services, reclamation and realistic synthesis remain feasible and potentially decision-changing. E09 should compare grouping eligibility/indexing against repeated preview; E14/E15 retain exact shared output representation and materialization questions. E04's asynchronous occurrence-local cache and E05's named families remain independent alternatives, not follow-ups conditional on optimizing this engine first.

Plausible refinements of owned terms, support operations and grouping are worth investigating, but polishing all of them before the other representations receive a first probe would bias the portfolio. The next first probe is E04. No broad direction is closed and no owner decision is needed for that experimental implementation.

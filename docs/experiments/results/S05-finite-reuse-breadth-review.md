## Why the resource phase is next

The current capacity solver infers an entire closed source: domain producers, a consuming `need/token` rule and a final failure rule. Its `Prepared::new` inspects that entire list, and `solve` returns complete answers. An ordinary suffix therefore needs a new source boundary and transport argument; simply treating those answers as a continuation is not established.

A successful gate could make direct consuming-resource elimination usable within a broader execution path, changing which runtime responsibilities remain necessary. A counterexample could instead show that interference, progress or identity obligations limit that elimination. Both outcomes affect architecture and language capability rather than another constant in a tested cache.

The strongest alternative is adaptive restoration/reunion: its existing late-link adverse case supplies concrete signals, and a policy could change the need for retained sharing. It requires policy design and independent progress/ownership checks. Resource composition has a source-derived solver, independent finite relation and ordinary caller controls ready; the missing work is a specific source-phase correspondence. That combination of architectural consequence and bounded implementation effort makes it next. This is a priority judgment, not negative evidence about adaptation.

Local resource claims and richer theories are also distinct under-investigated mechanisms. They require respectively a conflict/cancellation protocol and an independently specified denotation. They remain required and must be compared again at the selected gate. Familiarity with the capacity implementation does not establish their inferiority.

# Investigate integrated eligibility after the call-reuse pilot

Investigate token-independent eligibility discovery next under T072. The call-reuse pilot now establishes a large scoped cost problem against Direct, so another unchanged timing matrix is less informative than testing a distinct mechanism that could remove observed duplicated discovery. This is a priority decision, not a rejection of broader reuse.

## Why switch investigations here

The [lifecycle pilot](S05-inert-lifecycle.md) completes 20,118 measurements and retains all failures and skips. In 181 clock-qualified memo/Direct scenarios, median paired ratios range from 2.02× to 84.21×. Even zero measured disposal leaves every median ratio above 1.66×. More repetitions could refine these magnitudes, but the immediate decision does not depend on distinguishing a near-threshold advantage: the current implementation does not yet supply a competitive complete candidate on those sources. Small clock-sensitive cases remain unclassified.

Cheaper recognition remains a consequential alternative. The prior allocation study cannot establish its runtime potential, and a disposal bound cannot settle key construction or transport. A meaningful follow-up needs a sound compact/incremental identity or coarser call recognition, with fresh results and invalidating near misses. That is a mechanism change, not simply confirmation of the present matrix. Retain it under T075 and reconsider it at the next integrated result or obstruction.

The strongest ready distinct alternative is identified by the [integrated-selection ownership study](S02-selection-ownership.md). On six flat source requests, its protocol performs 91 eligibility discoveries and absorbs 70 duplicates; only six requests commit. Token multiplicity causes repeated discovery. A changed eligibility organization could eliminate that work while preserving stable source selection, equality dependencies and consuming effects. Existing independent source traces and local/conventional controls make this a bounded implementation experiment.

## The next useful result

Implement and qualify eligibility discovery whose repeated work does not scale with identical token multiplicity. Preserve nonwinning requests until they can commit, source precedence, equality-enabled readiness, duplicate resources, failure and cancellation. Removing a token head without checking later eligibility and selection is not sufficient. Compare against the existing protocol and competent local/ordinary controls on flat, sparse, equality-enabled and competing requests, including a no-benefit case.

First require complete independent answer/trace correspondence and evidence that the intended duplicate discovery actually disappears. Then charge changed preparation, encoding, retained state, execution and disposal before drawing an efficiency conclusion. Reuse applicable gates; do not restart an entire baseline programme. Register exact implementation-dependent configurations and bounds before comparative runs. The first source/work result or obstruction is the next selection boundary, not permission for an indefinite series of prerequisites.

## Alternatives and review obligations

Demand discovery/copy repair remains important: it could change confirmed favorable and adverse regimes, but already has ordinary timing against strong controls. Coarser reuse now has a clear adverse implementation to improve upon. Integrated eligibility takes precedence because its particular repeated work has an independent causal trace and its changed organization remains untested. These judgments compare likely decision value and implementation effort; they are not measured architecture rankings.

Complete-path integration should proceed when a surviving mechanism changes a credible composed candidate. The present memo path does not justify prioritizing its integration over resolving its large local cost. Favorable demand and integrated paths still need composition, language-boundary and sustained-lifetime comparisons. Compact projection, matching/subscriptions, restoration, distinct choice calculi, compilation, output and native/parallel questions remain required through the [sequence](../next-cycle.md).

## Why the next package is worth doing first

Actual local pull-tabbing is a strong ready alternative: it could change how demand distributes work and remains distinct from suspended calls. Contextual deduction reuse and strategic port rewriting could change equality ownership more substantially than the current maps. Broader source-derived lowering could eliminate interpreter work beyond these regular sources. None is rejected or postponed on a performance claim.

The selected package requires an exact-schema derivation and repeated runs of an already validated harness. It can determine whether the observed peak reduction comes with a supported runtime tradeoff, and whether a cheaper direct control dominates this particular source contract. Those answers affect whether extending this contextual implementation is valuable at all. Implementing another source organization before establishing them would leave a consequential control gap at modest avoidable investigation cost.

Register the complete comparison after the lowering correctness gate. Retain adverse no-choice, update-heavy and consuming cases. If timings overlap, obtain enough evidence to bound the decision rather than treating overlap as a loss. If a consequential defect appears, perform a bounded attribution check and revisit selection. A verified direct-control advantage limits the tested schema; it cannot reject contextual ownership on general sources.

Reconsider direction immediately after that comparison. Do not automatically tune the contextual implementation or broaden its syntax. The distinct integrated, pull-tab and lowering mechanisms above remain candidates for the next selection, and their present status remains unfinished.

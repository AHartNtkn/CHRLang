## Remaining investigations: decision value test

Independent reviews challenged both broad contextual-lowering deferral and the storage disposition. Their concrete questions, rather than a general desire for more experiments, determine the next selection.

- **Contextual finite lowering:** could extend elimination of source selection beyond sealed entries. It needs source-priority, shared-binding, external-observer, failure and finite-service correspondence. Existing counterexamples identify why naive contraction fails; they do not prove no broader certificate exists. The audit must decide whether widening the admitted set changes the recommended organization enough to justify that semantic gate.
- **Trailing/replay and search storage:** could reduce explicit branch copying, especially on deep restoration paths. E01 establishes that snapshot storage savings and physical repeated work are different; T048 does not rank all storage designs. Any closure disposition must retain this limitation and explain why the current baseline recommendation does not depend on copying being optimal.
- **Maintained joins/adaptive access:** could reduce repeated matching under a different update/selectivity regime. Corrected anchors already avoid the demonstrated unavailable-key scan; collision and repair controls expose maintenance counterpressure. Another experiment needs a cost that could change the selected execution organization, rather than merely improve an access policy.
- **Integrated search or direct choice graphs:** could combine equality and alternative ownership more tightly. Current ordinary integration shows real overlap with higher ownership costs; conditional execution already demonstrates direct supported source effects. Broader graph correctness requires resource, failure and publication gates. Neither current prototype maturity nor its absence decides its value.
- **Conditional reclamation/publication:** could broaden ongoing use by addressing retained history. Finite disposal is insufficient; alias cutoffs and sibling-delay witnesses remain contrary evidence. A new ownership protocol needs a regime where successful reclamation could reverse the surviving explicit-stream recommendation.
- **Native generation/solver learning:** larger reuse or coupled search could reverse particular backend costs. Existing direct paths already eliminate much machinery; current compilation does not amortize against Direct and small complete enumeration disfavors Boolean solving. No universal compiler or solver ordering is implied.
- **Workers and broader programs:** warm pools, larger granularity or connected ownership could change deployment-specific results. Current parallel ordering overlaps and supplied labels do not establish a deployment or workload distribution. Further work needs a computational contrast, not a target favorable timing.

## Audit result and selected T050

The full goal is **not yet complete**. Current `Core::fork_clone` copies the arena and ordinary store, pools, dispatch, history, pending work, queue, dependency and index containers. The mixed comparison has four choices and a small state. The stream cases likewise have small semantic continuations. E01 demonstrates persistence savings, but its largest memory case retained large full residual answer batches. These do not answer how to preserve substantial live state across cheap alternatives that mostly fail and emit no answer.

T050 therefore selects a bounded state-preservation screen before implementing a new storage engine. Construct ordinary source work that builds substantial live state, branches with little mutation, rejects most alternatives and eventually produces one small complete answer. Vary live-state size independently of alternative count, with no-choice and all-success controls. Independent expected observations must establish that payload state is semantically real and appropriately consumed, rather than omitted from answers. Account for preparation, fork/storage work, mutation/failure, observation and disposal separately; do not use diagnostic counters as primary timings.

If copied-state ownership is consequential, select one credible snapshot strategy for a prospectively registered matched comparison. If matching, source cleanup or publication dominates, use that causal evidence to decide whether storage redesign is worth doing. This is a screen for an architecture choice, not a commitment to trail, replay or persistent maps. Fair service, pending bodies, fresh identities, bindings, indexes and propagation history remain part of the restoration contract.

The strongest alternative is now more specific than arbitrary contextual recursion: the current positive sharing workload contains an equation-free carrier countdown. A source-derived contraction could avoid that repeated work while retaining the gate boundary, potentially changing the sharing interpretation. Names must not be recognized specially; unknown controls, external observers, multiple occurrences and source priority need explicit correspondence. Consuming/reposting a carrier changes occurrence order, so a pure body alone is not proof that eager contraction preserves competing gates. A bounded semantic gate remains a credible follow-up.

The storage screen comes first because it can alter the general explicit-search representation over arbitrary substantial state and has a lower semantic entry cost than a new contextual transformation. The carrier gate initially affects a particular optimizable source shape; even success would not establish elimination of all opaque shared work. Neither investigation is chosen because a prototype already exists. Reassess both after the screen; do not dismiss the carrier possibility merely because it is second.

Other directions retain the bounded limits above. No numerical worker ranking, universal solver/compiler choice or all-language impossibility is needed to select T050. No external resource or owner decision blocks it. Adoption choices remain the owner's; further authorized evidence gathering continues.


## Review and ordering judgment

The storage/lifetime reviewer recommends the state-preservation screen first. The access/lowering reviewer recommends the carrier certificate first, because it can challenge the only currently separated favorable sharing placement with a small semantic gate. Both reject closure now. Root selects storage first: identifying its magnitude under small output can change the default search-state organization, whereas carrier contraction initially qualifies a pure countdown regime. Source inspection proves copying occurs, not that it dominates; that missing attribution is exactly the bounded screen. This qualitative priority is a judgment about breadth and entry cost, not a measured utility score. Reassess after its evidence rather than treating the alternative as a mandatory later implementation.

The carrier reviewer also supplies a plausible narrow certificate: start at an ordinarily selected step, admit one live carrier occurrence and a finite spine, require opaque unchanged payloads and no observers/interior competing cases, stop before the terminal body, and retain occurrence-identity accounting and resumable search service. These premises make the lead concrete without proving or adopting the transformation in this audit.

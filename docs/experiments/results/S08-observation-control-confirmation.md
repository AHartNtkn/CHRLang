## Allocation and ownership also matter

In that growing-output example, templates request 5,317,775 primary bytes, specialization 5,319,752, and exact-source elimination 10,250,522. Peak requested growth is respectively 866,866, 866,200 and 1,703,968 bytes. The exact-source control's lower median time therefore does not establish total efficiency superiority. Its additional allocation is another implementation/ownership question if it becomes consequential to selecting that path.

The comparison retains complete owned output trees. It does not establish costs for compact observations, long-lived consumers, general compiler artifacts or arbitrary CHR programs. Template entry, ground-key and construction restrictions remain. Exact-source elimination recognizes this source family; it is a credible cost control, not proof of general lowering.

Primary time includes preparation, changed-query setup, complete execution/observation, engine/answer disposal and prepared disposal. Source/input-inclusive and first-answer measurements are separate. Meter timings are excluded from speed claims. Requested heap allocation is not RSS; native compilation is excluded. Counts are pointwise descriptions with seven paired blocks and no workload weights or population-wide claims.
